a letter

From your Shareek-e-Hayat

Faria, there is a word people use loosely — "girlfriend" — and it never once fit you, because you were never that to me. You are my fiancée, my future wife, the one I chose on purpose and would choose again in every life I'm given. You are the only woman I call close to my heart in that way, not because there was no one else in the world, but because there was never anyone else I wanted.

Your soul found its way into mine so quietly I almost didn't notice the exact day it happened — only that one morning I woke up and it was simply true. Your heart, baccha, has been mine to hold since, and I have tried, every day, to hold it the way something that precious deserves to be held.

You call me Shareek-e-Hayat — the one who shares your life — and I have never been given a title I am prouder to carry. Today, you turn nineteen. I just wanted you to open your eyes to something made only for you, before anyone else in the world gets to say happy birthday.

I'm still here in Bangladesh with you right now, but I won't be for long — I'll be leaving soon, and fourteen thousand kilometers will sit between us for a while after that. In sha Allah, we will not measure this love in kilometers forever. In sha Allah, we will be together — properly, permanently — and this distance will only ever be a chapter we look back on, not the whole story.

This year, you are my fiancée. In sha Allah, next year on this very day, you will be my wife — and I will get to spend every birthday after this one calling you that instead.

— your Kafi

14,000 kilometers, in sha Allah, is temporary

Right now I'm still here, baccha — still in Bangladesh, still close enough to reach you. But I'll be leaving soon, and that's the distance I'm already dreading before it's even happened. An ocean, a few countries, a very long flight between us. It was never something I worried about long-term, though — it's just a number, and in sha Allah, a temporary one. Because one day, not too far from now, you won't be waiting on the other side of it — you'll be right there with me, for good.
